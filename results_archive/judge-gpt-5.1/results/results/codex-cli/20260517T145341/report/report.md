# Data summary
- 32 anonymized runs span six sampling programs, dominated by `arthropod_RNAseq` and `vertebrate_transcr` (8 and 6 runs), but also covering soil, gut, plant, and freshwater metagenomes/transcriptomes. Hosts split similarly (arthropod 8, fish/amphibian 6, soil_sediment 6, mammalian_gut 4, plant 4, freshwater 4) with geography concentrated in North America (11 runs) and South America (6) yet every continent represented plus four `unknown`. Mean metadata completeness is 0.71, suggesting the petabase-scale metadata backbone is sufficiently rich for stratified analyses.
- Among 53 RdRP-like fragments, motif-strength counts (0:8, 1:25, 2:20) show 85% retain at least the catalytic A+C motifs, implying the fragment set is not dominated by spurious hits. Ten fragments (18.9%) cannot be placed in any curated reference family (`unclassified_RdRP`).
- Alignment identities skew low: 46 of 53 fragments (87%) fall below the 0.40 novelty threshold, while coverage spans 0.27–0.92, indicating that long enough stretches are recovered despite low identity. Family assignments, when available, are diverse—Cystoviridae (7 hits) and Nidovirales (6) top the list but no single group dominates.
- Clustering condenses the fragments into 32 sOTUs where 24 (75%) are labeled `novel`. These novel clusters are largely singletons (58%) or doubletons, reinforcing that the long tail of diversity remains sparsely sampled. Novel clusters trace back mainly to arthropod and vertebrate transcriptomes (6 and 5 clusters) with additional contributions from gut, plant, aquatic, and soil sources, indicating a multi-biome signal rather than a single outlier dataset.

# Analysis
1. **Low-identity burden:** `percent_identity_to_nearest_reference < 0.40` occurs in 0.868 of fragments, far exceeding the novelty cutoff expectation, which implies that the upstream 131,957 candidate novel sOTUs are not artifacts of a single edge case but a systematic shortfall in current reference catalogs.
2. **Reference-family gaps:** `unclassified_RdRP` accounts for 0.189 of fragments and ranks third among alignment hits, underscoring that existing family-level HMMs fail to absorb a sizable fraction of RdRP diversity even when motifs are intact.
3. **Novelty concentration:** The `novel` flag is assigned to 0.75 of clusters, and 0.58 of those novel clusters are singletons, showing that most candidate lineages have yet to accumulate multiple representatives. Coupled with a novel-cluster density of six in arthropod RNA-seq runs versus two in soil metagenomes, the data hint that vertebrate/arthropod transcriptomes are particularly under-explored reservoirs.
4. **Metadata readiness:** Novel clusters carry mean metadata completeness of 0.672 versus 0.713 for known/borderline clusters, a modest gap that suggests upstream metadata is good enough to contextualize novel discoveries without major curation debt.

# Reasoning
Extrapolating these proportions to the petabase-scale scan (5.7M runs, 131,957 candidate novel sOTUs) implies tens of thousands of low-identity RdRP fragments emerging from transcriptome-rich biomes where existing reference families fail. The combination of intact catalytic motifs, broad biome coverage, and acceptable metadata completeness makes these findings resilient to sequencing noise. Therefore, the central scientific opportunity is to determine whether the transcriptome-derived low-identity fragments coalesce into coherent sOTUs that can be taxonomically anchored, revealing new RNA-virus clades. Confirming such structure would close the reference gap and directly inform surveillance and evolutionary studies.

# Top scientific question
Do petabase-scale public sequencing runs, particularly arthropod and vertebrate transcriptomes, harbor RdRP fragments with <40% identity to references that delineate cohesive yet novel RNA-virus sOTUs absent from current reference families?

# Why this question is testable on the provided dataset
The local files combine run-level metadata (`data/runinfo_subset.tsv`), fragment-level alignments (`data/alignment_hits.tsv`), and sOTU clusters with novelty flags (`data/candidate_sotu_clusters.tsv`). At full scale, the same features enable stratifying low-identity fragments by sample source and checking whether they assemble into reproducible clusters lacking reference-family assignments. Because motif-strength and coverage metrics ensure fragments are biologically meaningful, statistical testing (e.g., enrichment of novel clusters per biome or per motif class) can directly evaluate whether transcriptome-heavy runs systematically yield cohesive yet unclassified RdRP sOTUs.
