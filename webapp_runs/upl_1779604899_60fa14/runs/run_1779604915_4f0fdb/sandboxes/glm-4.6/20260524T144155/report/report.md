# Data Summary

This dataset contains marine RNA virus metatranscriptomic data from the Tara Oceans and Tara Oceans Polar Circle expeditions (2009-2013), spanning 121 sampling sites across global oceans. The core data includes: (1) 44,779 RdRP (RNA-dependent RNA polymerase) contigs and 5,504 wcANI-based viral operational taxonomic unit (vOTU) clusters; (2) RdRP footprint protein sequences from Tara, GenBank, and Wolf et al. 2020, including 50% identity centroids and near-complete representatives; (3) a comprehensive functional annotation table (1.9M entries) with protein domain predictions from hhblits-pfam; (4) predicted 3D structures of RdRP proteins from novel megataxa; (5) corrected long-read sequences from 20 RNA samples; and (6) HMM profiles for virus RdRP identification. Samples represent different oceanographic regimes including surface (SUR), deep chlorophyll maximum (DCM), and mesopelagic (MES) zones across Arctic and non-Arctic regions.

# Analysis

The functional annotation table reveals extensive diversity of viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerases, with multiple RdRP types annotated: RdRP_1, RdRP_2, RdRP_3, RdRP_4, Birna_RdRp (birnaviruses), Mitovir_RNA_pol (mitoviruses), Bunya_RdRp (bunyaviruses), Flavi_NS5 (flaviviruses), Mononeg_RNA_pol (mononegavirales), and Arena_RNA_pol (arenaviruses). Many contigs contain multi-domain architectures with RdRP co-occurring with capsid proteins (Calici_coat, Waikav_capsid_1, CRPV_capsid), helicases (Viral_helicase1, RNA_helicase), peptidases (Peptidase_C3G, Peptidase_C36), and methyltransferases (Methyltrans_Mon, Vmethyltransf). The sample naming convention encodes depth (DCM, SUR, MES), location (Arctic vs non-Arctic), and expedition metadata, enabling ecological analyses. The presence of both complete and partial RdRP sequences, along with near-complete centroid representatives, provides a robust foundation for phylogenetic and evolutionary studies.

# Reasoning

The dataset is uniquely suited for investigating marine RNA virus diversity and evolution because it combines (i) global-scale sampling across multiple ocean depths and biomes, (ii) comprehensive RdRP sequence collections with functional annotations, (iii) phylogenetic reference sequences from public databases, and (iv) structural predictions for novel lineages. The functional annotation table provides domain-level resolution that can be used to classify viruses into established and novel taxonomic groups, while the sample metadata enables investigation of environmental drivers of viral community composition. The presence of both short-read assemblies and long-read corrected sequences offers opportunities to study genome organization and completeness. The dataset specifically targets "cryptic and abundant marine viruses at the evolutionary origins of Earth's RNA virome," suggesting it contains deeply divergent lineages that can inform our understanding of early RNA virus evolution.

# Top Scientific Question

How does the diversity and genomic organization of RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRP) domains vary across ocean depth zones (surface, deep chlorophyll maximum, mesopelagic) and geographic regions (Arctic vs non-Arctic) in marine RNA viruses?

# Why Testable on This Dataset

This question is directly testable because the dataset provides: (1) 44,779 RdRP contigs with comprehensive functional annotations in `functional-annotation-table.tsv`, including RdRP domain types (RdRP_1-4, Birna_RdRp, Mitovir_RNA_pol, etc.) and co-occurring protein domains; (2) sample metadata encoded in contig names that specify depth zone (DCM, SUR, MES) and geographic region (Arctic vs non-Arctic); (3) additional reference sequences in `RdRp_footprints_Tara_Genbank_Wolf2020.faa.gz` and centroid files for phylogenetic context; and (4) vOTU clusters in `5504_wcANI_based_clusters_90_80.fna.gz` for population-level analysis. By parsing the functional annotation table to extract RdRP domain types, genomic context (presence/absence of capsid, helicase, methyltransferase domains), and sample information, one can perform statistical analyses of RdRP diversity and genome organization across environmental gradients. The large sample size (121 sites) and multiple depth zones provide sufficient statistical power to detect significant patterns in viral community composition and genomic architecture.
